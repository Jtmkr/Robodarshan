What is a Motor?

This lesson covers the basics of motors: how they work, how to control them, and how to pick the right one for your robot. We'll look at DC motors, gear motors, motor drivers (especially the L298N), and servo motors. By the end, you'll know how to wire up a motor to your microcontroller and make it spin!

A motor is a device that converts electrical energy → mechanical motion (rotation). Almost every robot needs motors — they're what make wheels spin, arms move, and grippers open and close.

Why Robots Need Motors

  • Locomotion — wheels, tracks, legs
  • Manipulation — arms, grippers
  • Actuation — valves, pumps, fans
  • Precision — positioning, steering

Energy Conversion

Input: Electrical energy (voltage + current)

Output: Mechanical energy (torque + RPM)

Key idea

More current → more torque. More voltage → more speed (roughly).

DC Motor — Working Principle

A DC motor works on the principle that a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field experiences a force (Lorentz Force). This force creates torque and makes the shaft rotate.

The diagram above shows the basic sequence — current through the coil creates a magnetic interaction that produces rotational force on the shaft.

Direction of Rotation

  • Determined by polarity of voltage applied
  • Swap +ve and −ve → motor reverses
  • Use an H-Bridge circuit to reverse electronically

Speed Control

  • Speed ∝ Applied Voltage
  • Use PWM (Pulse Width Modulation) from microcontroller
  • Higher duty cycle = faster rotation

DC Gear Motor

How DC GEAR MOTOR Works with ARDUINO and L298N (YouTube, 10:04)

A DC gear motor is a regular DC motor with a gearbox attached to its output shaft. The gearbox trades speed for torque — super useful in robotics!

What Are Gears?

Interlocking toothed wheels that transfer rotational motion. Different sizes = different speed/torque.

Speed vs Torque

Gear reduction lowers RPM but increases torque. Perfect for moving heavy loads slowly.

Robotics Uses

Robot wheels, robotic arms, conveyor belts, elevators — anywhere you need force, not just speed.

Gear Ratio Formula

Gear Ratio = N2 / N1

Where N1 = input gear teeth, N2 = output gear teeth.

Example: 40-tooth output ÷ 10-tooth input = 4:1 ratio

  • Output speed = 1/4 of input speed
  • Output torque = 4× input torque

Quick Example

  • Motor: 1000 RPM, 0.5 N·m torque
  • Gear ratio: 5:1
  • Output speed = 1000 ÷ 5 = 200 RPM
  • Output torque = 0.5 × 5 = 2.5 N·m

Motor Driver — Why Do We Need It?

Motor Driver in depth | H-Bridge | L293D, L298N, TB6612FNG, VNH2S… (YouTube, 12:50)

You cannot connect a motor directly to an Arduino or ESP32. Here's why — and what to do about it.

Current Problem

Arduino GPIO pins can supply only ~40 mA max. A small DC motor needs 200 mA to several Amps. Direct connection = fried microcontroller!

Direction Problem

GPIO pins can only output HIGH or LOW — they can't reverse polarity to change motor direction. You need an H-Bridge circuit.

Protection

Motors generate back-EMF (voltage spikes) when they stop or change direction. Motor drivers have built-in protection diodes.

Warning

Never connect a motor directly to a microcontroller GPIO pin. Always use a motor driver module!

L298N Motor Driver — Pinout & Connections

Speed Control Tutorial of Single DC Geared Motor With L298N Motor Driver (YouTube, 10:40)

The L298N is a dual H-bridge motor driver module. It can control two DC motors (or one stepper motor) with direction and speed control. Very popular in beginner robotics!

Motor Supply (Vs)

5V – 35V DC. Connect your motor battery here. Not the same as logic power!

Logic Inputs (IN1–IN4)

Connect to Arduino GPIO. IN1/IN2 control Motor A. IN3/IN4 control Motor B. HIGH/LOW combinations set direction.

Motor Outputs (OUT1–OUT4)

OUT1 & OUT2 → Motor A. OUT3 & OUT4 → Motor B. Polarity here determines rotation direction.

Enable Pins & GND

ENA & ENB: Connect to PWM pins for speed control. Common GND between Arduino, driver, and motor supply is critical!

  • Motor Battery − → L298N GND
  • Motor Wire 1 → OUT1
  • Motor Wire 2 → OUT2
Common GND rule

Arduino GND, L298N GND, and motor battery GND must ALL be connected together!

Direction Control & H-Bridge Concept

The L298N contains an H-Bridge — a circuit of 4 switches (transistors) that can reverse current direction through the motor electronically.

IN1 / IN2 Truth Table (Motor A)

IN1IN2Motor State
LOWLOWStop (brake)
HIGHLOWForward
LOWHIGHReverse
HIGHHIGHStop (brake)

H-Bridge Visual

Think of 4 switches arranged like the letter H:

  • Top-left + Bottom-right ON → Forward
  • Top-right + Bottom-left ON → Reverse
  • All OFF → Motor coasts to stop

The L298N handles all this internally — you just set IN1/IN2!

Speed Control with PWM

Keep IN1/IN2 fixed for direction. Vary ENA using analogWrite() (0–255). Higher value = faster motor.

Servo Motor — Position Control

Servo Motors, how do they work? (YouTube, 15:12)

A servo motor is different from a DC motor — it doesn't spin continuously. Instead, it moves to a specific angle and holds that position. Great for robotic arms, steering, and precise movements!

Basic Components

  • DC motor (inside)
  • Gearbox (for torque)
  • Potentiometer (position feedback)
  • Control circuit (compares target vs actual)

Robotics Applications

  • Robotic arm joints
  • RC car steering
  • Camera pan/tilt
  • Flap and valve control

How Position Control Works

Servo receives a PWM signal on its signal wire:

  • 1 ms pulse → 0° position
  • 1.5 ms pulse → 90° (center)
  • 2 ms pulse → 180° position

Arduino Servo library handles this automatically with servo.write(angle).

Servo Wire Colors

  • Red → VCC (5V)
  • Brown/Black → GND
  • Orange/Yellow → Signal (PWM pin)

Motor Selection Checklist

Before buying a motor for your robot, run through this checklist. Picking the wrong motor is one of the most common beginner mistakes!

  1. Required Speed (RPM) — How fast does the wheel/arm need to move? Calculate from desired robot speed and wheel diameter.
  2. Required Torque (N·m) — How much force is needed? Consider robot weight, friction, and incline. Add a 2× safety margin!
  3. Supply Voltage — Match motor voltage to your battery. Common: 3V, 6V, 12V. Don't exceed motor rating.
  4. Current Draw — Check stall current. Your driver and battery must handle it. Add a fuse for safety.
  5. Load & Weight — Heavier load = more torque needed. Gear motors help, but don't overload the motor.
  6. Gear Ratio — Need more torque? Use a higher gear ratio. Need speed? Lower ratio. Balance both!
Lesson 10 Summary

Motors convert electricity to motion. Use gear motors for torque. Always use a motor driver (L298N). Control direction with IN pins, speed with PWM. Use servos for precise angles. Check the checklist before buying!
