What is a Robot?

Welcome to your first robotics lesson! These notes cover the absolute basics — what a robot is, why we use them, and how the core system of Sensors → Controller → Actuators works together. No advanced math here, just clear concepts to get you started.

A robot is a machine that can sense its environment, think (process information), and act on its own — usually under some level of computer control. Robots can be programmed to do tasks automatically, with varying degrees of independence.

The word "robot" comes from the Czech word robota, meaning "forced labor." The term was first used in a 1920 play by Karel Čapek. Today, robots range from simple toy bots to complex surgical machines!

Key idea

A robot is NOT just any machine — it must be able to sense, decide, and act on its own!

Quick Definition

A programmable machine that:

  • Senses its surroundings
  • Processes information
  • Takes physical action
  • Can be reprogrammed for new tasks

Robotics is the branch of engineering and science that deals with the design, construction, operation, and application of robots. It combines mechanical engineering, electronics, and computer science.

Why Do We Use Robots?

Robots are used in many fields because they can do things that are dangerous, repetitive, or too precise for humans. Here are the main reasons:

Speed & Efficiency

Robots work faster than humans for repetitive tasks — no breaks, no fatigue!

Safety

Handle dangerous jobs like bomb disposal, toxic waste cleanup, or deep-sea exploration.

Precision

Robotic arms in surgery or manufacturing can achieve accuracy humans simply cannot match.

Repetition

Assembly lines use robots for tasks repeated thousands of times without errors.

Sensors — The Robot's "Eyes & Ears"

Sensors collect information from the robot's surroundings and convert it into electrical signals the controller can understand. Without sensors, a robot is "blind" and "deaf"!

Common Sensor Types

Ultrasonic Sensor

Measures distance using sound waves (like a bat!)

Light / IR Sensor

Detects light intensity or infrared signals

Temperature Sensor

Reads ambient or component temperature

Touch / Bumper Sensor

Detects physical contact or pressure

Remember

Sensors = INPUT to the system. They feed data into the controller.

Controller / Microcontroller — The "Brain"

The controller (or microcontroller) is the decision-making center of the robot. It reads data from sensors, runs your programmed instructions, and sends commands to actuators. Popular beginner boards include Arduino and ESP32.

What it does

  • Reads sensor inputs continuously
  • Runs your program (the "logic")
  • Makes decisions based on conditions
  • Sends output signals to actuators

Popular Boards for Beginners

  • Arduino Uno — Great starter board, huge community
  • ESP32 — More powerful, has WiFi & Bluetooth built-in
  • Raspberry Pi — Mini computer, runs full OS
Key Point

The controller is where your code lives. You write the logic that tells the robot how to respond to sensor data!

Actuators — The "Muscles"

Actuators are the components that produce physical movement or action. They receive electrical signals from the controller and convert them into motion — this is how the robot actually does things in the real world!

Common Actuator Types

DC Motor

Spins continuously — used for wheels

Servo Motor

Rotates to a specific angle — used for arms

Stepper Motor

Precise step-by-step rotation

LED / Buzzer

Simple output — light or sound

Remember

Actuators = OUTPUT of the system. They are how the robot affects the world!

Power Supply — The "Heart"

No power = no robot! The power supply provides electrical energy to all components. Choosing the right power source is a critical design decision.

Batteries

Portable and convenient. Common types: AA, Li-ion, LiPo. LiPo batteries are popular in drones and RC robots for high energy density.

Wall Adapter (DC)

Provides steady power from a wall outlet. Good for stationary robots or lab prototypes. Usually 5V or 12V.

Solar Panels

Used in outdoor or long-duration robots. Converts sunlight to electricity. Often paired with a rechargeable battery.

Voltage Regulators

Ensure components receive the correct voltage (e.g., 5V for Arduino). Protects sensitive electronics from damage.

Types & Applications of Robots

Robots come in many shapes and sizes depending on where and how they're used. Here's a quick overview of the major categories:

Industrial Robots

Welding, painting, assembly, and packaging in factories. Examples: robotic arms, CNC machines.

Domestic / Service Robots

Robot vacuum cleaners (Roomba), lawn mowers, and companion robots for home use.

Educational Robots

LEGO Mindstorms, Arduino-based robots, line followers — used to teach STEM concepts.

Medical Robots

Surgical robots (like da Vinci), rehabilitation robots, and drug delivery systems.
