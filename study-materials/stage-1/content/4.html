What is a breadboard?

A breadboard turns circuit ideas into quick experiments. It is a temporary circuit-building surface. Parts plug into holes that connect internally, so you can move, test, and rearrange them without soldering or damaging them.

Start with LEDs, switches, resistors, and controllers.

The center gap and terminal strips

Terminal strips run in connected groups on each side of the center gap. Holes in one five-hole group are electrically connected; holes across the gap are not. Power rails run along the edges and distribute VCC/+ and GND/−.

Power rails

Use the red or + rail for VCC/+ and the blue, black, or − rail for GND/−. Run jumpers from a rail to any terminal strip that needs power.

Jumper wires

Jumper wires are the temporary connections between parts. Choose a wire long enough to route cleanly. Press each end firmly into place.

A basic circuit: source, control, load

Energy starts at the source, travels through a controlled path, and reaches the load. The breadboard gives every part a temporary place to connect.

Example: press the switch to light the LED

Build the path in order. The LED lights only when the circuit is closed through the intended route.

  • Press closes the path.
  • Release opens the path.

Common mistakes to catch before powering up

Power off, trace the path, then test again.

  • Wrong rail connection — check that VCC/+ and GND/− are on the intended rails.
  • Short circuit — never connect positive directly to ground; inspect the path first.
  • Loose jumper — reseat both ends if the circuit flickers or does nothing.
  • Reversed LED polarity — anode faces the resistor; cathode returns to GND/−.
